# Creative Brief
## Elevator Pitch
*The Mansion of Maroy* is a four act narrative cosmic horror tabletop RPG. It is built up from the Liminal Horror core ruleset for streamlined gameplay, with changes to stress and fallout mechanics that allow for different play styles, as well as a factional system with different endings. Set in the remote Faroe Islands during 1935, players explore a cursed island of cultists praying to a God whose dreams are being made real. Players will experience an epic journey into the unknown, where it isn't about how you lose sanity, but how you wield the weird.

## Purpose of Product
* Provide a four act adventure narrative set in 1935, inspired by the culture of the Faroe Islands and isolation and similar games like Call of Cthulhu: Masks of Nyarlathotep and Silent Hill
* Offer fans of historical cosmic horror a fresh setting with a unique atmosphere. From remote villages and a cursed mansion estate to a dimension-hopping climactic clash with an original pantheon
* Develop on Liminal Horror core ruleset for a rules-lite game system with a focus on non-punitive sanity mechanics, balancing narrative depth and faction-based choices to enhance replayability
* Design a comprehensive, well-organized product that includes the narrative, Facilitator and Investigator rules, maps, and handouts; available in both digital PDF and Paperback formats and as an optional hardback for collectors

## Mood
It’s 1935 and the world is teetering on the brink of collapse. Across Europe and beyond, tensions are rising, and the shadows of conflict loom larger each day. Nations grapple with man-made nightmares of political upheaval, violence, and fear. Yet, in the far north, within the isolated and windswept Faroe Islands, another horror stirs—something ancient, something powerful enough to alter reality itself.

The mood of _The Mansion of Maroy_ is one of creeping dread and living nightmares, where dreams of survival are forged in uncaring conditions. It is about cosmic horror on the grandest scale, where strength is found in confronting the unknown and embracing change is the only path forward. It is about loss, love, and sacrifice in a windswept and isolated landscape, where jagged cliffs and fog-cloaked valleys conceal horrors born of an angry God. Where the rugged villages and their peoples feel haunted, as if something unseen stirs behind them like a puppet-master.

Players step into this world burdened by unease, where the lines between reality and nightmares blur. Every whisper of the wind, every shadow that moves in the fog hints at forces far older and more powerful than humanity can comprehend. Maroy ('Mara' for Spirit and 'Oy' for Island) is a place where the past never stays buried, and those who seek the truth will become transformed by it.

## Themes
* Nightmare Horror: Beneath the veil of Maroy lies the terrifying power of ancient, unknowable gods. As Investigators delve deeper into the island’s mysteries, each step brings them closer to a truth more horrifying than the last. Cult secrets, hidden motives, and the looming dread of awakening something too powerful to contain drives the narrative.
* Isolation and Survival: The remote, storm-swept Maroy Island is dotted with factions at a stalemate. It is up to the Investigators which sides they choose and who will be left to aid them in the difficult times ahead. Each faction is bound with hatred for the others, and further divided by those that seek a different path. This sense of isolation will make each NPC encounter more intimate, allowing for more nuanced characters and greater impact from choices made.
* Dreams vs. Reality: The island exists in a state where dreams and nightmares bleed into the real world. This theme permeates the game in literal ways as players encounter surreal landscapes, shifting realities, and the creeping suspicion that not everything they see can be trusted.
* Sanity: Rather than punishing the loss of sanity during traumatic events, the game explores fallout as power, offering new abilities at a cost. Characters must grapple with the trade-off in playstyles between gaining control over the unreality of Maroy and maintaining their grip on what’s real.
* Checkpoint Sandbox: The game will be structured in chapters that develop over the course of critical decision points within broader acts. Investigators will be guided by the Facilitator through checkpoints in these chapters that serve to keep the narrative going, however what the players do within these locations will be more sandbox oriented. Side-quests and encounters will be available on these open-world maps through discovery and chance, while main-quests will help keep overarching goals at the forefront of Investigators minds.

## Writing Goals
* Utilise Liminal Horror's set of rules so that the game is easy to pick up and play, but have enough depth that combat and exploration is engaging
* Write the lore, history, and political factions so that Facilitators could ignore the narrative and produce their own stories within the setting themselves
* Write a canon 4-act narrative with a prologue and epilogue, exploring the island, the pantheon, and a final climatic confrontation that provides multiple endings based on critical choices the players make during the game
* Define a list of engaging and layered NPCs and a bestiary that drive the narrative forward, including suggested dialogue and tips for Facilitators
* Create puzzles that are engaging and fun to figure out
* Write in a style that is easy to read and comprehend, avoiding language that is overly verbose or idiosyncratic

## Illustration Goals
* Create a visual identity for the game that is easily recognisable and simple, focusing on the themes of bright nightmares and leaking unreality in the periphery
* Draw visual inspiration from the 1930s black-and-white print styles of artists like Elinborg Lützen, Käthe Kollwitz, Frans Masereel, Lynd Ward, and Rockwell Kent. [Associated Pinterest board.](https://pin.it/3RvjbXqIh)
* Focus on stylised figurative elements to evoke a light mystical connection to place and people, adding elements that unnerve the viewer or hint at something ancient and evil
* Establish the remote setting of the Faroe Islands and other dimensions through illustrations of significant locations and events in the narrative
* Create illustrative examples of aspects related to the game within the world, such as a puffin on a skull etc. that can be added into the layout as required.
    * Square shaped
    * Panoramic images
    * Vertically rectangular
    * Full Bleed
* Illustrate the lore and history of the game, setting out the tone of the setting and locations
* Create pre-established player and major NPC portraits
* Create maps, handout clues, and other material needed to run the game
* Create book detailing where appropriate based on the visual language of the book design

## Awesome Things
* A unique blend of historical and cosmic horror. Set against the backdrop of 1935, _The Mansion of Maroy_ merges the real-world culture of the Faroe Islands with a new Pantheon that threatens to unravel the world
* In a world where nightmares may hold the key to survival, losing your sanity can unlock powerful new abilities—but at what cost? The game’s non-punitive sanity system allows players to embrace the madness while still shaping their own fate.
* Choose your allies and enemies carefully. The ancient gods, secretive cults, and desperate survivors all vie for control. Every decision has consequences, and the factions you side with can alter the course of the story.
* Uncover a chilling mystery across four interconnected acts, from battling dark forces aboard a cursed ship to exploring haunted mansions and the nightmare realms it harbours
